# What is needed to conform

For each standard: the paragraphs to cite, the core requirements, the documents normally expected, and typical ESAP actions. Paragraph numbers are from the 2012 Performance Standards (1 January 2012) and were checked against the text when rule set v1.2.0 was written. "GNn-m" means Guidance Note n, note m. If the IFC documents are uploaded, confirm the paragraph in the text before quoting it; `paragraph-map.md` says where to look.

Scale everything to the category. A Category C company needs policy, labour and OHS basics, not an ESIA.

## PS1 Assessment and management of E&S risks and impacts
Cite: scope paras 4-5; policy para 6; identification of risks and impacts paras 7-12 (area of influence including associated facilities and cumulative impacts para 8; third-party risks para 9; primary supply chains para 10; related plans and studies para 11; disadvantaged or vulnerable groups para 12); management programmes and Action Plans paras 13-16; organisational capacity and competence paras 17-19; emergency preparedness paras 20-21; monitoring and review paras 22-24; stakeholder engagement paras 25-33 (SEP para 27, disclosure para 29, consultation para 30, informed consultation and participation para 31); external communications and grievance mechanism paras 34-35; reporting to Affected Communities para 36.
Requirements: an E&S policy; a risk and impact identification process by competent professionals, with external experts where impacts are potentially significant or technically complex (para 19); management programmes with measurable Action Plans; an organisational structure with competent staff; emergency preparedness; monitoring and senior-management review; stakeholder engagement scaled to impacts, with informed consultation and participation where impacts are significant; a grievance mechanism for Affected Communities; periodic reporting to them.
Documents: E&S policy; ESIA or focused E&S assessment; ESMP; ESMS manual; Stakeholder Engagement Plan; grievance mechanism procedure; emergency response plan; monitoring and reporting procedure.
ESAP actions: adopt policy; appoint E&S lead; commission ESIA or assessment; develop ESMS; prepare SEP and grievance mechanism; annual E&S reporting to the investor.

## PS2 Labour and working conditions
Cite: scope paras 5-7; HR policies and terms paras 8-9; collective bargaining para 10; migrant workers para 11; accommodation para 12; workers' organisations paras 13-14; non-discrimination paras 15-17; retrenchment paras 18-19; grievance mechanism para 20; child labour para 21; forced labour para 22; occupational health and safety para 23; workers engaged by third parties paras 24-26; supply chain paras 27-29. Guidance: GN2-32 (accommodation guidance), GN2-71 to GN2-73 (identity documents, debt, freedom of movement).
Requirements: written HR policies and terms; compliance with national law and respect for freedom of association where law allows; equal opportunity; analysis of alternatives and consultation before collective dismissals, with notice and severance; a worker grievance mechanism (extended to contracted workers where the third party has none, para 26); no economically exploitative child labour and no forced labour; a safe and healthy workplace; commercially reasonable efforts to oversee contractors; supply chain action where there is a high risk of child or forced labour or serious safety issues in the primary supply chain (client's leverage decides how far it can go, para 29).
Documents: HR policy and manual; OHS management plan; worker grievance procedure; contractor management procedure with PS2 clauses; worker accommodation standards (IFC/EBRD guidance) where camps are provided; retrenchment plan; supply chain risk review.
ESAP actions: update HR policy; establish worker grievance mechanism open to contract workers; OHS system with incident reporting; PS2 clauses in contracts and contractor audits; accommodation audit; supply chain mapping.

## PS3 Resource efficiency and pollution prevention
Cite: ambient conditions and good international industry practice para 4; EHS Guidelines para 5 (and the Performance Standards overview, paras 6-7, on applying the more stringent of the Guidelines and host-country rules); resource efficiency para 6; greenhouse gases paras 7-8; water para 9; pollution prevention paras 10-11; wastes para 12; hazardous materials para 13; pesticides paras 14-17.
Requirements: technically and financially feasible, cost-effective resource efficiency measures; feasible, cost-effective options to reduce project-related GHG emissions (para 7), and annual quantification of direct emissions and emissions from purchased energy where the project is expected to produce more than 25,000 tCO2e a year (para 8; the paragraph does not itself require public reporting); water efficiency where use is significant; avoiding or minimising pollutant release and waste, with the waste hierarchy; hazardous materials management; integrated pest management, with no WHO Class Ia or Ib pesticides and Class II only with controls and trained handlers (para 17).
Documents: pollution prevention and waste management plans; hazardous materials management plan; GHG quantification; water balance and water management plan; IPM plan; environmental monitoring programme.
ESAP actions: gap assessment against EHS Guidelines; GHG quantification and reduction options; monitoring of emissions and effluent; hazardous waste contracts with authorised handlers.

## PS4 Community health, safety and security
Cite: evaluating risks para 5; infrastructure and equipment design and safety para 6; hazardous materials para 7; ecosystem services para 8; water-borne and other diseases para 9; communicable diseases and labour influx para 10; emergency preparedness para 11; private security para 12; government security para 13; allegations of abuse para 14. Guidance: GN4-18 to GN4-20 (communicable disease, including with labour influx).
Requirements: assess risks to communities and set controls; design, build, operate and decommission structures to good international industry practice by competent professionals, certified or approved by competent authorities or professionals; where dams, tailings dams or ash ponds are in high-risk locations and failure could threaten communities, one or more external experts separate from the designers and builders review the structure from early development through decommissioning (para 6); avoid or minimise exposure to hazardous materials and control the safety of deliveries and waste transport; avoid road incidents; manage community exposure to disease, including the disease risk of labour influx; emergency preparedness with communities and authorities; assess security risks, make reasonable inquiries that guards are not implicated in past abuses, train them, allow force only for preventive and defensive purposes in proportion to the threat, and give communities a grievance mechanism (para 12); assess and document risks of government security forces (para 13).
Not in the PS4 text, but common lender expectations (say so when using them): the Voluntary Principles on Security and Human Rights as a security benchmark; controls on gender-based violence and sexual exploitation linked to labour influx; a worker code of conduct.
Documents: community health, safety and security plan; traffic management plan; emergency response plan shared with communities; security risk assessment and security management plan; labour influx management plan; structural safety plan and external expert review where dams, tailings dams or ash ponds are involved.
ESAP actions: traffic plan; security assessment and guard training; worker code of conduct; emergency drills with communities; structural safety certification; external dam safety review.

## PS5 Land acquisition and involuntary resettlement
Cite: applicability paras 5-6 (compulsory acquisition; negotiated settlements where expropriation was the fallback; restrictions on access to resources; some evictions; communal and natural resources). Exclusions in para 6: voluntary transactions, and impacts where land use is not changing. Project design para 8; compensation para 9; engagement para 10; grievance mechanism para 11; resettlement planning and census para 12; monitoring para 14; completion audit para 15; classification of displaced persons para 17; physical displacement paras 18-22; cut-off date and encroachers para 23; forced evictions para 24; economic displacement paras 25-29; government-managed resettlement paras 30-32.
Requirements: avoid or minimise displacement; a census and socio-economic baseline with a published cut-off date; compensation at full replacement cost; for people with no legal right or recognisable claim (para 17 iii): no payment for the land, but resettlement with security of tenure, compensation for non-land assets owned before the cut-off date, and livelihood restoration (para 22, footnote 8); livelihood restoration for economic displacement; engagement and a grievance mechanism early; where the government manages acquisition, describe its measures and prepare a supplemental plan for gaps (para 31); monitoring and a completion audit for significant displacement.
Documents: land acquisition and legacy review; census and socio-economic survey; Resettlement Action Plan (physical displacement); Livelihood Restoration Plan (economic displacement); supplemental plan where government-led; completion audit.
ESAP actions: legacy land audit; livelihood survey of informal users; RAP or LRP; land grievance mechanism; gap-filling top-up compensation where the government process falls short; proof of voluntariness for each negotiated parcel.

## PS6 Biodiversity conservation and sustainable management of living natural resources
Cite: scope paras 4-6; mitigation priority para 7; competent professionals para 8; mitigation hierarchy para 10; modified habitat paras 11-12; natural habitat paras 13-15; critical habitat paras 16-19 (definition para 16; conditions para 17; Biodiversity Action Plan and net gains para 18; offsets para 19); legally protected and internationally recognised areas para 20 (footnote 17 defines the internationally recognised areas); invasive alien species paras 21-23; ecosystem services paras 24-25; living natural resources paras 26-29; supply chain para 30.
Requirements: apply the mitigation hierarchy; in natural habitat, no significant conversion or degradation unless the para 14 conditions are met, and mitigate for no net loss; in critical habitat, no activity unless para 17 is demonstrated (no viable alternative on non-critical habitat; no measurable adverse impact on the qualifying biodiversity values and supporting processes; no net reduction in the population of any Critically Endangered or Endangered species; a long-term monitoring and evaluation programme), with mitigation set out in a Biodiversity Action Plan designed for net gains (para 18); in protected and internationally recognised areas, show the project is legally permitted, follow the area's management plan, consult managers and communities, and add conservation programmes (para 20); no intentional introduction of new alien species; maintain priority ecosystem services; credible sustainability standards for primary production of living natural resources; supply chain checks against conversion of natural or critical habitat.
Documents: biodiversity baseline; critical habitat assessment; Biodiversity Management Plan or Biodiversity Action Plan (net gain where critical habitat); offset design where residual impacts remain; ecosystem services review; supply chain sourcing policy.
ESAP actions: critical habitat assessment by a specialist; bird and bat surveys for wind and lines; bird diverters or undergrounding where needed; BAP; sourcing policy.

## PS7 Indigenous Peoples
Cite: applicability and definition paras 3-7; avoidance of adverse impacts and the Indigenous Peoples Plan paras 8-9; engagement, participation and consent paras 10-12 (para 12 explains FPIC); circumstances requiring FPIC paras 13-17 (land under traditional ownership or customary use para 14; relocation para 15; critical cultural heritage para 16; commercial use of cultural heritage para 17); mitigation and development benefits paras 18-20; government-managed processes paras 21-22.
Requirements: identify affected Indigenous communities (competent professionals may be needed, para 7); avoid adverse impacts or minimise and compensate; engagement through informed consultation and participation, involving their representative bodies and giving enough time for their decision making (para 10); FPIC, documented through good-faith negotiation, in the circumstances in paras 14-17; culturally appropriate benefits; a plan, or a broader community development plan with separate components (para 9); supplement government processes where needed.
Documents: Indigenous Peoples screening and social assessment; Indigenous Peoples Plan or Community Development Plan; documented FPIC process and agreement.
ESAP actions: specialist social assessment; engagement plan with traditional institutions and gram sabhas; FPIC process with independent facilitation where required; IPP.

## PS8 Cultural heritage
Cite: applicability para 5 (PS8 does not apply to Indigenous Peoples' cultural heritage; PS7 covers it); protection in design and execution para 6; competent professionals para 7; chance-find procedures para 8; consultation para 9; community access para 10; removal of replicable and non-replicable heritage paras 11-12; critical cultural heritage paras 13-14; legally protected areas para 15; project's commercial use of cultural heritage para 16.
Requirements: protect heritage under internationally recognised practices; retain competent professionals where there is a chance of impacts; site and design to avoid significant adverse impacts; a chance-find procedure in the ESMS where the assessment finds heritage is expected to be found, with no further disturbance of a find until competent professionals have assessed it (para 8); consult the communities who use the heritage and keep their access; strict conditions for removal; critical cultural heritage not to be removed, significantly altered or damaged except in exceptional circumstances, with documented good-faith consultation and external experts (para 14); compliance in legally protected areas; informed consultation, fair benefit sharing and no commercialisation without them where heritage is used commercially (para 16).
Documents: cultural heritage screening or impact assessment; chance-find procedure (where heritage is expected); heritage management plan where needed.
ESAP actions: heritage survey where indicators exist; chance-find procedure where heritage is expected, and as good practice in civil contracts with ground disturbance (not a PS8 requirement where nothing is expected); consultation with communities and heritage authorities.
