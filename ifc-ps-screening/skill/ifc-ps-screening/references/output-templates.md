# Output templates

## A. Quick screen (default in chat)

**Project:** [name, location, sector, stage]
**Provisional category:** [A / B / C / FI]. [One sentence on why.] Could move to [X] if [open fact].
**Exclusion List:** state "checked, no listed activity indicated" or name the item and the fact that decides it. Do not omit this line.
**Basis:** engine baseline, rule set v[x.y.z] (same as the checklist). List any Skill review changes here: "PS6 raised from NEEDS REVIEW to TRIGGERED because ...".
**Answers to check:** any sanity warnings the engine returned, and which answers they concern.

| PS | Verdict | Why (project facts) | What to do to conform | Source (PS paragraph) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| PS1 | TRIGGERED | ... | ... | PS1 para 6 |
| PS2 ... PS8 | | | | |

Mark a raised verdict in the Verdict column ("TRIGGERED (Skill review)"). An unmarked verdict is the engine's.

**Flagged for escalation:** each item, why it matters, who should look at it.
**India-law comparison** (or national law): one line per relevant standard; the Environmental Clearance point first if it applies.
**Open facts:** each UNKNOWN, with who should confirm it.
**Limitations:** First-pass screening only. It does not replace an ESDD, ESIA or ESMS, and the category is provisional.

Then offer: a Word screening memo, an ESAP draft, or a gap check of an uploaded document.

## B. Screening memo (Word document)

1. Header: project, location, date, prepared by (placeholder unless given), and the line "First-pass screening; not a full ESDD".
2. Executive summary (half a page): provisional category, the one or two facts most likely to change it, the most decision-relevant point (often the national law gate).
3. Project description: the intake facts in prose, then an explicit list of unknowns.
4. Standard-by-standard assessment: one Heading 2 per PS with verdict, a paragraph of reasoning, the trigger that applies, requirements with citations, and next steps.
5. Categorisation rationale: why this category and not the one above or below.
6. National law comparison: table of standard, domestic equivalent, gap.
7. Escalation and open facts: table of item, fact needed, owner.
8. Indicative ESAP (optional): table per template C.
9. Limitations.

Use real heading styles so a table of contents works. Expand into prose; do not paste the quick table.

## C. ESAP draft

| No. | PS | Action | Source | Deliverable | Responsibility | Timing | Acceptance criterion |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | PS1 | Adopt an E&S policy endorsed by the board | PS1 para 6 | Signed policy | CEO | Before first disbursement | Policy covers PS1 to PS8 commitments and is disclosed |

Start from the engine's actions for each triggered standard (they carry their paragraph in the Source column); add the deliverable, owner, timing and acceptance criterion. Label actions that are lender practice rather than Performance Standard text.

Timing uses financing milestones (before signing, before first disbursement, within N months of financial close, before construction, before operation). Acceptance criteria must be checkable.

## D. Gap check

| Requirement (PS and section) | What the document provides | Gap | Suggested fix | Priority |
|---|---|---|---|---|

Priority: High (blocks conformance or creates significant risk), Medium, Low. Summarise the top five gaps at the start.
