# India: domestic law compared with the Performance Standards

General knowledge, last reviewed October 2026; none of it was checked against statute text when rule set v1.2.0 was written, apart from the points on the EIA Notification schedule and the Labour Codes. Laws, rules and notifications are amended often; tell the user to verify the current text, and to take legal advice, before relying on any point. For each standard, the comparison asks: does Indian law already require something equivalent, and what do the Performance Standards add? The engine returns a one-line version of each row below (rule set `national_law.IN.by_ps`); keep your wording consistent with it.

## Headline point: projects outside Environmental Clearance
The EIA Notification 2006 requires prior Environmental Clearance only for listed activities and thresholds. Solar PV, wind power and transmission lines are not listed (checked against the consolidated schedule to March 2025; re-check for later amendments). For such projects, the Performance Standards, through the financier, may be the only framework requiring an E&S assessment at all. State this prominently. Also check: building and construction thresholds, highway expansion thresholds, industrial estates, and state-specific requirements; Consent to Establish and Operate still applies to most industrial units.

| PS | Indian equivalent (main instruments) | What the Performance Standards add |
|---|---|---|
| PS1 | EIA Notification 2006 (listed projects); CTE/CTO under the Water Act 1974 and Air Act 1981; BRSR disclosure for top listed companies | A management system (ESMS), assessment for unlisted projects, stakeholder engagement and grievance mechanism throughout the project life, area of influence, associated facilities, cumulative impacts |
| PS2 | The four Labour Codes (Wages; Industrial Relations; Social Security; Occupational Safety, Health and Working Conditions), in force from 21 November 2025, with state rules and transition provisions; Child and Adolescent Labour Act 1986; Bonded Labour System (Abolition) Act 1976; POSH Act 2013 | Oversight of contractor and third-party workers, worker grievance mechanism, retrenchment process with alternatives analysis, primary supply chain review, accommodation standards |
| PS3 | Water and Air Acts (CTE/CTO from the State Pollution Control Board); Environment (Protection) Act 1986 standards; Hazardous and Other Wastes Rules 2016; E-Waste, Plastic Waste and Battery Waste rules; CGWA permission for groundwater; Insecticides Act 1968 | EHS Guidelines benchmarks (more stringent of national or EHS), resource efficiency, GHG quantification above 25,000 tCO2e a year, IPM, WHO pesticide class restrictions |
| PS4 | MSIHC Rules 1989 for major accident hazard installations; Disaster Management Act 2005; Private Security Agencies (Regulation) Act 2005; Dam Safety Act 2021 | Community health and safety assessment for all projects, labour influx and GBV/SEA risk, security risk assessment and conduct expectations, emergency planning with communities |
| PS5 | RFCTLARR Act 2013 (government acquisition; R&R for affected families); state land laws and negotiated purchase policies; Electricity Act 2003 provisions for transmission right-of-way and compensation guidelines | Coverage of negotiated purchase where expropriation is the fallback (PS5 para 5), informal users (non-land assets, resettlement and livelihood restoration, not the land itself: para 22), livelihood restoration, replacement cost, legacy review, completion audit. Voluntary purchases are outside PS5 (para 6) |
| PS6 | Wildlife (Protection) Act 1972 (amended 2022); Van (Sanrakshan Evam Samvardhan) Adhiniyam 1980 (formerly Forest (Conservation) Act, amended 2023); eco-sensitive zone notifications; CRZ Notification 2019; Biological Diversity Act 2002 (amended 2023); Wetlands Rules 2017 | Natural and critical habitat tests regardless of legal designation, no net loss or net gain, ecosystem services, sustainable sourcing of living resources |
| PS7 | Fifth and Sixth Schedules; PESA Act 1996 (gram sabha consultation in Scheduled Areas); Forest Rights Act 2006 (recognition of individual and community forest rights; the gram sabha's role in forest diversion has been changed and contested since the 2022 forest conservation rules, so check the current position); RFCTLARR provisions for Scheduled Areas | Identification of Indigenous Peoples beyond Scheduled Areas, FPIC in defined circumstances with documented good-faith negotiation, Indigenous Peoples Plan |
| PS8 | AMASR Act 1958 (prohibited area 100 m and regulated area 200 m around centrally protected monuments, NMA permission); state heritage laws; Treasure Trove Act 1878 | Chance-find procedure, community and intangible heritage, community access, conditions on removal, benefit sharing for commercial use |

## Notes for the comparison line
- Write it as: "Indian law: [instrument] requires [X]; PS[n] adds [Y]." One line per standard.
- Where Indian law is stricter or more specific (for example AMASR distances, CRZ zoning), say so; the more stringent requirement applies.
- Compliance with Indian law is itself a PS1 requirement; non-compliance found during screening is an ESAP item.
