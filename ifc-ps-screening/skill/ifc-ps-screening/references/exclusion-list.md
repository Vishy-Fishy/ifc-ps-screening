# IFC Exclusion List

Generated from rule set v1.2.0. IFC Exclusion List (2007), as amended 8 December 2025. Source: https://www.ifc.org/exclusionlist (text supplied by EMC, 29 October 2026). Applies to transactions and engagements mandated after 31 December 2025.

The Exclusion List is a gate that comes before the Performance Standards. Activities on it are not financed by IFC (Sustainability Policy para 19), whatever the Performance Standards result. The engine output carries an `exclusion_check` reminder on every result. The engine cannot tell whether an activity is excluded: that needs the project facts, so you check it.

## How to use it

- At intake, compare the project's products, services, supply chain and sponsor activities with the list. If one matches, say so first, before any verdict, and name the item.
- Where the match is uncertain (for example a sponsor with a tobacco or alcohol arm, or asbestos in building materials), do not decide: say which fact decides it (see note 1 and the asbestos exception) and who should confirm.
- The reasonableness test is IFC's to apply, not yours or the client's. Do not suggest a project qualifies.
- The list was amended on 8 December 2025 to remove ‘production or trade in radioactive materials’. An older copy that still lists radioactive materials is out of date.

## IFC does not finance the following projects:

1. Production or trade in any product or activity deemed illegal under host country laws or regulations or international conventions and agreements, or subject to international bans, such as pharmaceuticals, pesticides/herbicides, ozone depleting substances, PCB’s, wildlife or products regulated under CITES.
2. Production or trade in weapons and munitions. (note 1)
3. Production or trade in alcoholic beverages (excluding beer and wine). (note 1)
4. Production or trade in tobacco. (note 1)
5. Gambling, casinos and equivalent enterprises. (note 1)
6. Production or trade in unbonded asbestos fibers. This does not apply to purchase and use of bonded asbestos cement sheeting where the asbestos content is less than 20%.
7. Drift net fishing in the marine environment using nets in excess of 2.5 km. in length.

- **Note 1.** This does not apply to project sponsors who are not substantially involved in these activities. “Not substantially involved” means that the activity concerned is ancillary to a project sponsor’s primary operations.
- **Note 2.** Forced labor means all work or service, not voluntarily performed, that is extracted from an individual under threat of force or penalty.
- **Note 3.** Harmful child labor means the employment of children that is economically exploitive, or is likely to be hazardous to, or to interfere with, the child’s education, or to be harmful to the child’s health, or physical, mental, spiritual, moral, or social development.

A reasonableness test will be applied when the activities of the project company would have a significant development impact but circumstances of the country require adjustment to the Exclusion List.

## Financial intermediaries

**All financial intermediaries, except microfinance and trade finance below, apply these in addition to the Exclusion List:**

1. Production or activities involving harmful or exploitative forms of forced labor/harmful child labor. (note 2, 3)
2. Commercial logging operations for use in primary tropical moist forest.
3. Production or trade in wood or other forestry products other than from sustainably managed forests.

**When investing in microfinance activities, FIs apply these in addition to the Exclusion List:**

1. Production or activities involving harmful or exploitative forms of forced labor/harmful child labor. (note 2, 3)
2. Production, trade, storage, or transport of significant volumes of hazardous chemicals, or commercial scale usage of hazardous chemicals. Hazardous chemicals include gasoline, kerosene, and other petroleum products.
3. Production or activities that impinge on the lands owned, or claimed under adjudication, by Indigenous Peoples, without full documented consent of such peoples.

**For trade finance projects, FIs apply this in addition to the Exclusion List:**

1. Production or activities involving harmful or exploitative forms of forced labor/harmful child labor. (note 2, 3)

For an FI, “substantially involved” (footnote 1) is generally taken to mean a cumulative portfolio exposure above 5 percent to investees or borrowers that earn more than 20 percent of their revenues from an excluded activity (for multi-seller e-commerce platforms, a gross merchandise value indicator replaces the revenue test). The 5 percent threshold would not generally apply to private equity funds. FIs that serve only individual retail clients (mortgages, student loans) are generally not required to apply the Exclusion List. (FI Guidance Note para 7 and fn 8–10, 31)

## Other restrictions

IFC may also restrict activities beyond this list in a project’s legal documents. For FI projects with a defined use of proceeds it excludes coal-related sub-projects (coal mining, coal transport, coal-fired power plants and infrastructure dedicated to them) and trade finance for thermal coal (FI Guidance Note para 7 and fn 11).
